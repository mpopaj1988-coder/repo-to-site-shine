The myth that you can't beach in Florida in winter is one of those things that only people who don't live here believe. Daytime highs in December and January routinely hit the mid-70s, the sun is bright, and the beach is yours. Here's how to actually enjoy it.

## What "Winter" Actually Means Here

Average daytime highs December through February: 71–73°F. Sunny days are common, often warmer than the average. The water sits around 65°F — cool for swimming but fine for wading, paddleboarding in a shorty wetsuit, or just being near. The sand is the same beautiful sand as July, with about a tenth of the people on it.

## What to Pack Differently

* **A light layer** — mornings and evenings can dip into the 50s
* **Long-sleeve UPF shirt** — sun is still strong; long-sleeve also handles the cooler air
* **A real towel and a windbreaker** — for the breeze off the water
* **Closed shoes for early walks** — sand at 7am in January is genuinely cold

## What's Actually Better in Winter

* **Empty beaches** — Caladesi and Fort De Soto on a January Tuesday are surreal
* **Better sunrises** — winter sunrises on the east-facing bay side are spectacular and the air is clear
* **Dolphins** — winter inshore waters bring more dolphin sightings, often close to shore
* **Bird life** — peak migration season; pelicans, herons, roseate spoonbills are everywhere
* **Sand sculpting** — the cooler weather makes long beach days actually enjoyable

## What's Trickier

* Beachfront restaurants sometimes close patios or run reduced hours
* Lifeguards are typically off-duty at most beaches in winter
* Cold fronts move through every 7–10 days, dropping highs into the 60s for a day or two
