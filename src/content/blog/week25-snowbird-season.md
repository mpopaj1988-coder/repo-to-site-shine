Snowbird season on the Gulf Coast officially kicks off in November and runs through April. If you're planning a month or longer in Florida this winter, the time to book was already a few months ago — but it's not too late if you know where to look.

## What Counts as a Snowbird Stay

Most owners and managers price differently for stays of 28 nights or more. A monthly snowbird rate is typically 30–50% lower per night than a one-week rate, which makes longer stays dramatically more affordable than people expect.

## What to Look For in a Long-Term Rental

After the first week, the things you didn't think mattered start to matter:

* **A real kitchen with real equipment** — not the bare-minimum tourist kitchen
* **In-unit laundry** — laundromat trips get old fast
* **Reliable wifi** — many snowbirds work or video-call family
* **A workspace or desk area** — even retirees end up needing one
* **Comfortable seating for actual evenings in** — not just a beach-day couch
* **Walking access to groceries** — driving for every errand wears thin

## Best Areas for Long Stays

* **St. Pete neighborhoods** — walkable, real downtown, great food and arts scene
* **Dunedin** — small town feel, brewery scene, easy beach access
* **Indian Rocks Beach** — beach-front but quieter than Clearwater
* **Largo / Seminole** — more space for the price, central to everything

## Practical Things People Forget

* Florida driver's license is not required for stays under 6 months, but you should let your auto insurance know
* Most snowbird rentals do not include cable TV — bring your streaming logins
* Mail forwarding through USPS works seamlessly for snowbird stays
* Set up grocery delivery (Instacart, Publix delivery) before you arrive — it cuts the first-week chaos significantly

## Booking Window

Prime January and February stays book out 6–9 months in advance. November and April are easier to secure. December has a holiday premium for the last two weeks of the month.
