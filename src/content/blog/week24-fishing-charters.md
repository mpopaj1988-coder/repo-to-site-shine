The Gulf Coast has some of the best year-round fishing in the country, and you don't need to own a boat or know what you're doing — charter captains will put you on fish. Here's how to actually book one without wasting money or ending up on the wrong trip.

## Inshore vs. Offshore — Pick the Right Trip

**Inshore** charters fish the bays, flats, and mangroves close to land. Calmer water, shorter runs, target species like snook, redfish, trout, and tarpon (in season). Better for beginners, kids, anyone prone to seasickness, and half-day budgets.

**Offshore** charters head into the Gulf for grouper, snapper, kingfish, and the occasional shark or mahi. Longer runs, rougher water, more expensive, more serious. The fish are bigger but the day is harder.

If it's your first charter or you're bringing kids, book inshore.

## Half-Day vs. Full-Day

Half-day inshore (4 hours) runs $400–600 for up to 4 people and is plenty for most casual trips. Full-day offshore (8 hours) starts around $1,200 and goes up. Don't book a full day for your first time out — you'll know by hour 5 if it was the right call.

## What's Usually Included

- Rods, reels, tackle, bait
- Florida fishing license (the boat's license covers you)
- Fish cleaning at the dock
- Coolers and ice

## What You Bring

- Sunscreen (reef-safe), polarized sunglasses, hat
- Closed-toe shoes or sandals with backstraps
- Snacks and drinks (most boats allow coolers)
- A small tip for the mate — 15–20% of the charter rate is standard

## Where to Launch From

St. Pete, Madeira Beach, Indian Rocks Beach, and Tarpon Springs all have well-established charter fleets.
